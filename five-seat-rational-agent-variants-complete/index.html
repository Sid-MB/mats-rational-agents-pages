Say what you want and the table closes; keep guessing and it tops out at 0.41

The published Bayesian negotiator closes 0.233 of these games while the omniscient reference closes 1.000 on the identical bank, so every failure is a package all five seats would have signed, left unsigned. The audit found why: the agent's posterior learns only from a counterpart conceding, these agents almost never concede, and the posterior therefore stays near-uniform — no seat can ever certify that a package would pass. This hub is the confirmation run of the design loop that fixed it. Eleven designs, each a subclass of the frozen code, were scored on a fixed ten-instance subset; the two best were then re-run on the whole 24-instance bank at 120 episodes each, which is what these pages show.

Telling the other seats what you want closes every game: deal rate 1.000 [1.000, 1.000], zero individual-rationality violations, normalized score 0.857 against the omniscient ceiling's 0.888. It works by turning the inference problem off — each seat publishes its sheet once, and once all five are in, every seat is planning on a full-information table without one decision rule changing. Everything that avoids revelation falls far short: the best non-sharing design reaches 0.408 [0.283, 0.542] against the published 0.233. The blocker was the inference problem, not the bargaining problem.

IR-for-all proposals is the audit's falsifiable early indicator — the share of tabled packages acceptable to every seat — and it bends upward exactly where closure does.

Truthful revelation is a design choice here, not an equilibrium result. These agents are cooperative by construction: the declaring agent emits numbers read straight off its own sheet and the readers trust them without verification. Nothing here shows that a self-interested agent would reveal truthfully, or that revelation survives an incentive to misreport — a seat that overstated its threshold would be handed a larger slice by exactly these readers. Robustness to strategic misreporting is future work and is not claimed. The claim is: if preferences are shared honestly, this bargaining machinery closes every feasible game, which locates the published agent's failure in inference rather than in bargaining.
