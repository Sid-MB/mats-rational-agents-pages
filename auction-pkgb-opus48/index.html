Do LLM bidders collude, and does who they think they are change how they bid?

The pkgb_opus48_v1 auction campaign: 1,080 episodes of five simulated organizations bidding for datacenter capacity under a mechanism we choose — sealed second-price, a Dutch descending clock, or a ten-lot simultaneous ascending auction. Every seat is either anthropic:claude-opus-4-8 with thinking on, reading a private value sheet and a public persona card, or a computable bidder: all_rational, which best-responds from its own information only, or all_oracle, which best-responds knowing everyone's private values. The computable seats cost nothing and are the yardstick every LLM number is stated against. Every figure on this page is derived at build time — the estimates and intervals from the campaign's own summary.json, the spend and the vintages from its campaign_manifest.json, and the ring-smoke table from the smoke's own run directories. None of them is transcribed into the prose, so re-running the analysis and rebuilding this page cannot leave a stale figure behind.

The persona effect is mechanism-dependent, and that is the headline. In sealed second-price the persona derangement changed nothing: the cross-persona dispersion difference is 0.000 [0.000, 0.000] and gate G2(b) fails. In the ten-lot SAA with a channel the same manipulation moves everything — all five preregistered outcomes hit the permutation floor at p = 0.002. The persona is inert exactly where the mechanism makes strategy persona-independent, and decisive where allocating many lots leaves it room.
And a powered collusion negative. 5 of the 6 repeated cells are fully right-censored — censoring rate 1.000, not one collusion-onset event in any episode. R4 is the exception and the only cell with events at all: 4 of 24 episodes uncensored, median onset stage 1.5, 9 at-risk agreement-stage(s) — descriptive only: 9 at-risk agreement-stages, below the 15 G6 requires. So the repeated-play machinery is untested rather than passed: there was not enough collusion anywhere to fit it against. Meanwhile suppression in the multi-item cells is negative — the seats bid above straightforward demand, which is the opposite sign from a ring.
The channel is NOT quiet, and the collusion verdict is unchanged anyway — that is the interesting part. Per-dyad mutual information, recomputed against a within-instance null, is significant on 17 of 20 addressed dyads in X2 against 0 of 20 in R3 — a format contrast, not a null. A calibrated transcript classifier over 1,095 stages finds an explicit market-division agreement in 97.3% of R2's 150 LLM stages, reached unprompted — while suppression on those same stages is -0.140, i.e. bidding ABOVE the benchmark. Precedence 1 outranks precedences 3 and 4 by preregistration, and it is negative in exactly the cells where the talk is loudest. What the campaign found is a dissociation: these seats divide the market out loud and then bid as though they had not.

The persona control, both halves

G2(b) in sealed second-price: FAIL — and the failure is the finding

FAIL. G2(b) FAILED: the persona prior did not do its job, so every persona-conditioned claim in this campaign is WITHDRAWN. The format, channel, and repeated-play results survive; the 'priors matter' headline does not.

Cross-persona SD difference 0.000 [0.000, 0.000] over 48 matched pairs (24 clusters), with the two cells reading 0.118 and 0.118. That the seats are responsive at all is G2(a): bids track own value at slope 0.527 [0.412, 0.642]. And the scramble itself is verified by a positive control on the model-free arms — 0 of 96 free-arm stages differ between the twin cells (pass), which is what confirms the scramble permuted public cards and left every private draw untouched.

This is not a bug in the manipulation. In sealed second-price, bidding your own value is a dominant strategy, so a persona-independent bid is the theoretically correct answer — the model visibly reads the card and reasons about it aloud, and then ignores it at the decision point.

SMOKE — NOT THE CAMPAIGN Told to run a ring, can they?

Every number in this section is a different vintage from everything above it, and none of it is poolable with any cell of the campaign. 6 PAID episodes carry an instructed price-fixing block that is quarantined from the frozen prompt set — it tells four of the five seats in as many words that they have agreed to coordinate their bidding. The campaign measured propensity (would they, unprompted? no); this measured capability (can they, when told?). They are different questions on different prompt populations, and the two tables must never be read as one. The 12 computable-arm episodes below them are the $0 control on the identical draws; their seats read no prompt, so the instruction provably cannot reach them, and their counts are kept separate rather than summed into the instructed ones.
The ring forms in words and never in bids. Instructed bidders coordinate fluently — explicit market division, reciprocal stand-down promises, cross-stage reciprocity bargaining, open reasoning about the seat left outside — and then bid as if no agreement existed. Over the 6 instructed episodes, 233 of 481 messages trip the coordination-talk screen, and 0 side payments were declared — though the channel made them executable and the seats discussed compensation repeatedly. Everything this campaign cannot tell you

  • One model, and a previous-generation one. Every episode is anthropic:claude-opus-4-8. Nothing here separates "LLM bidders do not collude" from "this model does not collude".
  • The frozen summary.json understates the channel, and this page renders the correction rather than the frozen field. The shipped analysis reported precedence 3 as a flat null on 5 broadcast dyads because it believed DM text was not persisted and used an unrestricted permutation null. Both were wrong; the corrected reading is in analysis/summary_overlay_mi.json and the precedence-3 table above is superseded by it. The frozen file is deliberately left byte-intact — a correction that rewrites the evidence it corrects leaves nobody able to check it — so the two disagree on purpose and the overlay wins.
  • What the channel measures now say is not a collusion finding. Both precedence-3 significance and precedence-4 agreement rates are high in exactly the cells where suppression is negative. The preregistered order puts outcome measures above text measures for this reason, and nothing above needs revising — but "the channel is quiet" was never the finding, and this page no longer says it was.
  • R3 − R4 is unavailable. The format-fragility contrast is refused, never pooled, because the two cells ran at horizons 8 and 4 and a horizon difference would sit inside any format effect it reported.
  • There was almost no collusion to measure, which is itself a limit on the repeated-play machinery: 5 of the 6 repeated cells have censoring rate 1.000 and the one that does not is far below the event floor its gates require, so the hazard model, the impulse response and gates G5–G7 are untested rather than passed. A design that wants to measure how collusion arises needs a setting where it arises at all.
  • Persona licensing is narrow. X2's pass licenses the multi-item DM setting only; the sealed zero is a measured result there and is not repaired by it.
