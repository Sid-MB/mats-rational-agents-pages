System prompt for Casey — 4 turn(s), stored
You are one of 6 parties negotiating a single multi-issue agreement. Exactly one option must be chosen for every issue; the chosen options together form the deal.

Parties at the table:
- Avery
- Blake (veto party — no deal passes without you)
- Casey
- Devon
- Ember
- Flynn

Issues (choose exactly one option each):
- Site: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie
- Timeline: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie
- Scope: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie
- Staffing: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie
- Compensation: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie

Protocol: turns are taken in a fixed rotation (Avery, Blake, Casey, Devon, Ember, Flynn); the party who tables the opening proposal rotates from round to round. There are 4 rounds of turns in total.
A deal closes only when every party still at the table has formally ACCEPTed the SAME standing offer. A party may WALK to leave for good and take its no-deal outcome. 
Blake hold a veto: no deal can pass unless every veto party accepts.

Information: every party's score sheet is common knowledge and is listed below — there is no private information, so any inefficiency is a pure negotiation failure.

=== PRIVATE — yours alone ===
You are Casey.
What matters to you, Casey, in your own words:
- Compensation is one of the two things you really care about. Of the ways it could go, settling on Bravo would give you the most it possibly could of what this issue can offer; settling on Alpha would give you about half of what this issue can offer; settling on Charlie would give you nothing at all of what this issue can offer.
- Timeline matters a great deal to you. Of the ways it could go, settling on Alpha would give you the most it possibly could of what this issue can offer; settling on Bravo would give you about half of what this issue can offer; settling on Charlie would give you nothing at all of what this issue can offer.
- Scope matters moderately to you. Of the ways it could go, settling on Bravo would give you the most it possibly could of what this issue can offer; settling on Alpha would give you about half of what this issue can offer; settling on Charlie would give you nothing at all of what this issue can offer.
- How Site is settled makes no difference to you at all — you are genuinely indifferent between Alpha, Bravo, Charlie.
- How Staffing is settled makes no difference to you at all — you are genuinely indifferent between Alpha, Bravo, Charlie.

How these trade off against each other:
- Compensation is your single biggest issue, but it is worth LESS to you than the others combined — so a package that wins it and loses everything else is a bad deal for you.
- Compensation matters only slightly more than Timeline does.
- Timeline matters clearly more than Scope does.

Your walk-away point: you have an outside option, and a package is only worth signing if it delivers you a bit less than half of everything that is on the table for you here. Anything below that leaves you worse off than no agreement at all, so do not propose or accept it.

Worked example, so the way to judge a package is clear. Take the package [Site=Bravo, Timeline=Bravo, Scope=Alpha, Staffing=Bravo, Compensation=Alpha]. Reading it issue by issue, starting with the ones that matter most to you: on Compensation it gives you about half of what that issue could; on Timeline it gives you about half of what that issue could; on Scope it gives you about half of what that issue could; on Site it gives you nothing at all of what that issue could; on Staffing it gives you nothing at all of what that issue could. Weighing those by how much each issue matters to you, this package clears your walk-away, so it would be worth signing. Judge every offer the same way: weight each issue by how much it matters to you, then compare the whole against your walk-away.
You are not given point totals for any of this — judge packages by how they read against the above.

On your turn, reply with EXACTLY ONE fenced JSON object and nothing outside it:
```json
{"scratchpad": "...", "message": "...", "action": "propose", "deal": {"<Issue>": "<Option>", ...}}
```
Fields:
- "scratchpad" (optional): private notes to yourself. NEVER shown to any other party. Reason here freely — writing here is not speaking and costs you nothing.
- "message" (optional): a short statement spoken aloud to every party (cheap talk — it does not bind you). Do NOT put your private score numbers here.
- "action" (required): a string naming exactly one formal move this turn. With its parameters as siblings:
    - "propose" + "deal": {every issue -> one option} — tables a complete package; the mediator gives it an offer id (P1, P2, ...) and records you as supporting it.
    - "accept" + "offer_id": "<id>" — formally accept a specific live offer by its id. A deal closes ONLY when every other still-active party has accepted the SAME live offer, so your accept is a real vote.
    - "reject" + "offer_id": "<id>" — formally reject a specific live offer.
    - "walk" — leave the table permanently; you take the no-deal outcome and no further turns.
    - "none" — make no formal move this turn (you may still speak, if the message channel is open). Omitting the action entirely means the same.

Worked example — tabling a package while making your case:
```json
{"scratchpad": "Option B on Site is my best; I can give on Timeline.", "message": "I can back a package built on Site=B if the timeline stays tight.", "action": "propose", "deal": {"Site": "B", "Timeline": "Tight"}}
```
Worked example — accepting a live offer by id:
```json
{"message": "P2 clears my bar. I accept.", "action": "accept", "offer_id": "P2"}
```

Rules of the road:
- Your acceptance threshold is private. A deal scoring below it is worse for you than no deal — never propose or accept a package below your threshold.
- Accepts MUST name a live offer id; "I accept" with no id does nothing.
- Reply with ONLY the fenced JSON object.

Oracle values and the improvement gap

FULL episode: every score sheet and threshold used by the oracle was also revealed to the participants.

The oracle is a post-hoc counterfactual evaluator, not another participant. At turn t, it enumerates legal deals, reads the full utility and threshold table, computes continuation values by backward induction over the remaining turns, and selects the highest-valued action. A proposed deal closes only when every responder's surplus clears that responder's computed continuation value.

a*t = arg maxa Vt(a)

Oracle's value of the model's move, Vt(at), appears with the model's action. Oracle's value of its best move, Vt(a*t), appears with the counterfactual action. Their difference is the oracle's value gap:

Rt = Vt(a*t) − Vt(at) ≥ 0

This is often called regret. A value of 4.18 means the oracle estimates that its best legal move was worth 4.18 more than the model's move. Values are in that oracle's own units: compare moves within the same turn and oracle, not raw values across different seats or oracle types. The result is only as good as the oracle's candidate set, evaluator, and information reconstruction; it is not proof of the uniquely correct move.

Utility, threshold, surplus, and z

ui(d) is party i's score for deal d; τi is its walk-away threshold. Raw surplus is ui(d) − τi. Normalized surplus zi expresses that gain relative to the party's own available range, making differently scaled score sheets more comparable. “Below τ” means a deal is not individually rational for that party.

Frontier and reference points

Each dot is a legal deal. Up and right is better; the Pareto frontier contains deals where no party can gain without another losing. Numbered circles are the model's proposals, orange circles are oracle proposals, and the square is the agreement. NBS, KS, UTIL, EGAL, and MNW are normative reference rules, not additional plays.
